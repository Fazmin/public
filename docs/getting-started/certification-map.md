# Certification Map

Information security certifications are credentials awarded to individuals who demonstrate proficiency and expertise in various aspects of information security. These certifications are usually obtained by passing rigorous exams that test knowledge, skills, and competencies in specific areas of cybersecurity.

Certifications are helpful measures of proficiency, but they are not a substitute for experience, training, or demonstrable skill. More than anything, pursuing a certificate provides structure to information security learning goals. Each has a curriculum, defined learning goals, and types of forward-looking support, such as requirements to perform continuing education over a calendar year. If you are looking for structure in your security learning, a certificate may be for you.

## Recommended Certifications for Information Security Professionals

These certifications are highly recommended for professionals looking to further solidify their expertise and advance their careers in information security. They provide specialized knowledge and can enhance your proficiency in critical areas of cybersecurity. To help learners navigate their journey, they are broken down by experience level.

### Foundational & Entry-Level Certifications

These certifications are ideal for those just starting out in cybersecurity, career changers, or IT professionals looking to transition into a security-focused role.

#### ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC)
The CC is a true entry-level certification from ISC2 designed for individuals with no prior IT or cybersecurity experience. It covers fundamental concepts such as security principles, business continuity, disaster recovery, incident response, and network security. It is an excellent starting point for anyone looking to prove their foundational knowledge.

#### CompTIA Security+
Security+ is a globally recognized, vendor-neutral entry-level certification that covers core security concepts, including risk management, network security, and cryptography. While often pursued early in a career, it remains a heavily requested baseline certification for professionals looking to reinforce their foundational understanding of information security principles.

#### Microsoft Certified: Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals (SC-900)
For those working in or looking to work in cloud environments—specifically Azure and Microsoft 365—this certification provides a strong foundation in how security, compliance, and identity solutions are handled across Microsoft platforms. 

#### GIAC Information Security Fundamentals (GISF)
The GISF is an entry-level certification that validates a learner's knowledge of basic computer functions and networks, introductory cryptography, and fundamental cybersecurity technologies. It is ideal for non-technical professionals, system administrators, and new security practitioners.

#### Cisco Certified CyberOps Associate
This certification focuses on the tactical, operational skills needed in a Security Operations Center (SOC). It is a great entry-level option for those who want to focus specifically on threat detection, network monitoring, and incident response rather than general security management.

### Intermediate & Advanced Certifications

These certifications are for practitioners who have spent some time in the field and are looking to validate operational, managerial, or specialized expertise.

#### Systems Security Certified Practitioner (SSCP)
Also from ISC2, the SSCP is designed for professionals focused on operational security roles and requires at least one year of proven experience. It demonstrates proficiency in implementing, monitoring, and administering security policies and procedures. This certification is particularly beneficial for individuals seeking to strengthen their hands-on knowledge in day-to-day security operations.

#### Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP)
The CISSP is a globally recognized, advanced certification that validates deep expertise in security management and operations. It covers critical domains such as risk management, security architecture, and network security, making it ideal for those looking to take on senior leadership or strategic roles in cybersecurity. (Requires a minimum of 5 years of experience).

#### Information Technology Infrastructure Library (ITIL)
ITIL provides a framework for IT service management, focusing on aligning IT services with business objectives. Though not purely cybersecurity-related, ITIL is valuable for professionals working in both IT and security management, offering best practices for managing IT services, incident resolution, and service continuity.
