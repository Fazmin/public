## New here?

The learning domains (above) represent a body of passive learning resources - textbooks, multimedia, guides, and documents; in other words, resources that are meant to be read or watched. They are complemented hands-on learning activities to reinforce and anchor concepts. These activities may include structured labs, or select capture-the-flag (CTF) events. For the latter, these parts of this site help explain CTF events and how to participate in them:

1. Read [What is a CTF?](getting-started/what-is-a-ctf.md).

## Intended audience

This site helps you move beyond isolated facts and understand how the major areas of security connect: how systems are designed to be secure, how networks and identities are protected, how weaknesses are found and tested, and how software is built and broken. Alongside the domain material, Capture the Flag (CTF) practice gives you a problem-based learning approach to applying knowledge and networking with fellow defenders. These resources are designed for:

- Learners building depth across the cybersecurity domains
- Preparing for new responsibilities, work opportunities, or personal projects
- Team members preparing for infosec competitions and assessments
- Individuals pursuing certifications

## Responsible use

The material on this site is intended for learning and authorized practice. We teach security skills because defenders need to understand how attacks work. That knowledge comes with responsibility, and everyone who uses this site is asked to follow these principles.

**No shortcuts.** Take time to learn underlying technologies and their responsible and acceptable use. Read their documentation and ask for help with context and safe usage. Approach new technology with care and caution - the first goal of any simulation or competition is learning, not winning. Learning must be done responsibly when dangerous technologies are involved: many labs, CTFs, and cybersecurity skills and their associated tools can cause real harm if misused or abused.

**Know the rules before you start.** Computer misuse laws apply to learners too, and so do the policies of the networks you use. When you're unsure, ask first. You are responsible for knowing the laws where you are, and the acceptable-use policies of any network you connect to or technology used. 

**Permission first.** Only test systems you own or are explicitly authorised to test. A lab or CTF's published scope, a platform's rules, or a written agreement defines your permission; nothing else does. Being able to reach a system is not permission to touch it. Consent is a critical concept, and seeking consent first hinges on understanding what you're doing, and to whom. Experienced mentors and guides can help you avoid mistakes with new technologies and how they're used for a particular problem/challenge.

**Prevent and Contain potential mistakes.** Learn what it means to learn responsibly. Work in isolated labs, verify before you run anything, and keep your work recoverable. Our [lab setup guide](lab-setup.md) offers a high-level process. Your available options for isolation may vary based on the type of computer you're using, so make sure to seek a mentor if it's your first time.

**Stop and report.** If something goes wrong, or you find a real vulnerability, stop, write down what happened, and tell the right people: organisers, instructors, or the system's owner. Reporting early protects everyone. Help reinforce a culture of "us versus the problem" to allay any concerns of a member being blamed/shamed. We all make mistakes.

**Respect people.** Protect others' privacy, never publish personal information you come across, and treat fellow learners the way you'd want to be treated when you're the one asking for help, seeking guidance, or reporting an incident. There is never a bad time for professionalism, care, and compassion.

**Help us improve.** Our content is reviewed regularly, but some of it will go out of date. Check it against official documentation, and tell us at apd1280 at mcmaster dot ca when something's wrong.

Using this site means agreeing to these principles. See [Ethics and Rules](getting-started/ethics-and-rules.md) for additional notes on conduct.
