# Open Source Intelligence (OSINT)

Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) is the practice of collecting and analyzing information that is publicly and lawfully available, then turning it into something useful: a verified fact, a connection between entities, an assessment of risk.

[Social Media Profiling](social-media-profiling.md)
The practice of collecting, analyzing, and correlating publicly available information from social media platforms to construct a detailed profile of an individual or organization.

[Search Engine Exploitation](search-engine-exploitation.md)
Using advanced search operators and specialized queries ("dorking") to surface exposed files, login pages, and misconfigured servers that standard searches miss. The foundation for much of what follows, and the first place to audit an organization's own exposure.

[Metadata Analysis](metadata-analysis.md)
Extracting hidden data embedded in documents, images, and other files — authorship, timestamps, device and software details, sometimes location — to establish origin, verify authenticity, and detect what organizations unintentionally leak when they share files.

[DNS and WHOIS queries](dns-and-whois-queries.md)
Techniques used to gather detailed information about a domain’s registration, ownership, infrastructure, and associated network assets. 

[Code and Script Analysis](code-and-script-analysis.md)
Examining public source code, scripts, and commit histories for exposed credentials, API keys, internal logic, and revealing comments. Covers how secrets leak, why they persist in history after deletion, and how automated scanners find them.

[Geolocation and Mapping](geolocation-and-mapping.md)
Geolocation is the process of determining where a photo, video, or piece of information was captured or refers to — usually working from visual content alone, without relying on embedded location data.

[Subdomain Enumeration](subdomain-enumeration.md)
Discovering the full set of subdomains tied to a primary domain to map an organization's true attack surface, including legacy systems, staging environments, and the forgotten DNS records that lead to subdomain takeovers.

[Email Investigation](email-investigation.md)
Analyzing email headers, authentication results (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), and public references to an address to trace a message's origin, detect spoofing and phishing, and uncover linked accounts — while respecting the privacy weight of doing so.

[Historical Website Analysis](historical-website-analysis.md)
Using web archives like the Wayback Machine to recover deleted content, track how a site changed over time, and support forensic investigations — alongside a clear-eyed view of why snapshots are incomplete and what it takes to use them as evidence.

[Reverse Image Search](reverse-image-search.md)
Searching with an image rather than text to find where it appears, trace its origin, verify authenticity, and detect manipulation — distinguishing exact-image search from the distinct privacy concerns of facial-recognition search.

[Public Records and Data Sources](public-records-and-data-sources.md)
Mining government filings, court records, corporate registries, and open datasets for ownership trails, affiliations, and organizational structure, with emphasis on validating and cross-referencing findings and handling aggregated personal data responsibly.
